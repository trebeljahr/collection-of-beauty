A garden is nature with editing. Someone decided where the wild stops. Painters love gardens for exactly that reason. The editing is already half a composition before the easel even arrives.

The five gardens here are edited to wildly different ends. Bosch builds one no gardener could grow, a paradise that may be a trap. Runge shrinks the viewer to child height until a sunflower becomes a tree. Rubens throws a courtship party inside balustrades. Hiroshige hands the foreground to the irises and demotes the people. Young Monet tips his aunt's flowerbeds toward the sea like a box of colour with the lid off.

After Monet's pond swallowed the horizon in [Water gardens](/newsletter/0021-water-gardens), it is almost a relief to be back among hedges and fences. Gardens have edges. That is the whole comfort of them.
