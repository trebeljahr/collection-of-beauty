---

Bosch crowds the central panel until the garden cannot behave like a real place. Runge brings the same outdoor frame down to three children and one giant sunflower leaf.

Rubens turns a garden into a balustraded room of courtship and stone. Hiroshige cuts the view with iris stalks taller than his strolling visitors. Monet raises the horizon at Sainte-Adresse so the garden looks staged against the sea.

After Monet's late lily pond in [Water gardens](/newsletter/0021-water-gardens), these gardens have edges again: hedges, balustrades, iris beds and rolled lawn.
