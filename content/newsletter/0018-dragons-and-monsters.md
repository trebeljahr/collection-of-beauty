The legend says she leads the tamed beast to town, but most painters skip that part. Uccello paints the exact moment where the story is both a rescue and a dog walk. Saint George's storm cloud spirals behind him like it was drawn with a compass, the cave mouth is geometric, the dragon's wings carry neat target-mark roundels. Late Uccello is half fairy tale, half diagram."
  - id: "collection-of-beauty-hieronymus-bosch-triptych-of-temptation-of-st-anthony-wga2585"
    note: "Bosch's approach to monsters is volume. Saint Anthony kneels at the centre of the panel while an entire economy of hybrids gets on with its blasphemous business around him: fish that are also boats, jugs with legs, a burning village over there for atmosphere. The saint is almost lost in his own painting, which is the point. Temptation isn't one big devil. It's ten thousand small distractions. Sixteenth-century collectors couldn't get enough; copies multiplied across Europe immediately."
  - id: "collection-of-beauty-takiyasha-the-witch-and-the-skeleton-spectre"
    note: "The greatest reveal in Japanese printmaking: the sorceress Takiyasha reads her spell from a scroll, and the thing she has summoned through the ruined palace blinds is a skeleton the size of the building. Kuniyoshi runs it across all three sheets of the triptych, and the brilliant part is that he draws the skeleton with real anatomical accuracy, probably cribbed from imported Dutch medical books. Folklore monster, rendered with textbook bones. The single most metal image in the collection."

Horse and snake twist along the same diagonal, so tightly wound that the eye can't fully untangle them, with one hard theatrical spotlight on the panic. The Romantics didn't want monsters defeated. Every culture invents monsters, and what it invents tells on it. The fun in this issue is the five very different ideas of what a monster is for.

Sōtatsu's dragons are weather, powers you coexist with, painted half-dissolved in their own ink clouds. Uccello's dragon is a problem civilization solves, with lance and leash and geometry. Bosch's monsters are temptations, numberless and busy. Kuniyoshi's skeleton is a special effect with perfect anatomy, and Fuseli's serpent is the Romantic nightmare: the monster as something that happens to you too fast.

The demons in [Printed line](/newsletter/0014-printed-line) mobbed Saint Anthony in engraved black and white. Here the monsters get ink, oil, colour woodblock and a spotlight. They've done well for themselves.
