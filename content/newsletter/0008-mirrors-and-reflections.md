The painting contains a princess, her court, a dog, and the painter himself, and the room is upstaged by the blurriest object in it. Velázquez knew exactly what he was doing. Put a mirror in a painting and you've smuggled in a second painting. Painters have used the trick for centuries, and the variety of what they smuggle is the fun.

Caravaggio uses the water-mirror to close a doomed loop. Rubens uses a hand mirror to show a face and a back at once. Velázquez hides the two most powerful people in Spain inside the smallest, blurriest mirror he could paint. Utamaro and Ingres use glass for the most universal thing of all: checking the back of your own head.

The letters in [Books and reading](/newsletter/0004-books-and-reading) pulled a room's attention onto paper. Mirrors do the opposite trick. They fold the room back at you, and sometimes catch you standing there looking.
