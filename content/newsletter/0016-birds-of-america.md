Audubon shows seven birds on one branch at a moment when flocks of hundreds were still common in the Carolinas and Florida. Plate 26 is one of the early sheets of the folio."
---

The flamingo folds itself into the page. The heron does the same, neck and legs bent at right angles to fit the paper.

The hummingbirds solve the page by multiplying around a trumpet-creeper. The owl solves it by facing forward and trusting its facial discs. The Carolina parakeets fill the cocklebur branch with a colour no longer attached to any living North American flock.

Haeckel's marine specimens in [Microscopic ornament](/newsletter/0013-microscopic-ornament) were arranged for pattern. Audubon keeps the form life-size when he can, even when it costs him the sheet.
