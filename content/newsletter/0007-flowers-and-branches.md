Branches are the original composition machine. Give a painter one flowering stem and you get structure, rhythm, and a place for the birds to sit, all for free.

What I like about these five is how differently the same material behaves. Huizong's bamboo is discipline. Van Gogh's orchards are a sprint against the wind. Arcimboldo turns the same botany into a face. Rubens and Brueghel split one panel between friendship and flowers, and Sansetsu bends his branches into pure gold-ground theatre.

The Karasaki pine in [Japanese rain](/newsletter/0002-japanese-rain) held a whole storm inside one black silhouette. These branches hold finches, faces, orchards and a gold sky. Not bad for sticks.
