Most of art history's budget went to gods, kings and saints. This issue is for everyone else, the people carrying, cutting, herding and hauling at the edges of all those grander pictures, here moved to the middle.

Schongauer gives a market-bound family the engraved attention usually reserved for saints. Turner shrinks reapers into brushstrokes under a sky that dwarfs their earl. Rubens, done with ambition, watches a timber wagon come home. Van Gogh translates a shepherdess into his own colours from inside an asylum. Hokusai sets the tea harvest in front of Mount Fuji.

The horses in [Horses in motion](/newsletter/0012-horses-in-motion) charged and paraded. These ones pull wagons at a walk. Art needed a few thousand years to notice that the second kind moves the world.
