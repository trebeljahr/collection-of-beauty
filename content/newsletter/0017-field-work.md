    note: "A peasant family makes its way along a country road toward market, loaded with bundles and a basket. Martin Schongauer engraved the plate in Colmar around 1473 to 1475, before any sustained European tradition of dignified peasant imagery existed; his contemporaries usually framed the peasant as a comic type.     note: "A peasant cart loaded with felled timber rumbles down a rutted track as the last sun catches the clouds and treetops. Rubens painted the canvas in the last decade of his life, around 1635 to 1640, after he had bought the country estate of [Het Steen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Het_Steen) near Mechelen in 1635 and turned to private Flemish landscapes for his own pleasure. He kept these late landscapes in his own studio rather than selling them, and most passed only later into princely collections. The track and the load are the only worked surfaces in a wide late-afternoon landscape."

    note: "Van Gogh copies Millet's *La Bergère*, setting the shepherdess among her flock with a freer, broken surface. He painted the canvas during his year at the [Saint-Paul-de-Mausole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Paul-de-Mausole) asylum in Saint-Rémy in 1889, working from a black-and-white print of [Millet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Fran%C3%A7ois_Millet) because he had no access to the original. He wrote to Theo that copying Millet was 'translating into a different language', and the colours in the painting are his own invention. The staff fixes the shepherdess in the centre of the flock."

    note: "Mount Fuji rises beyond rows of tea plants and working figures, the foreground filled with women pressing leaves into bundles. Hokusai designed the print around 1830 to 1832 as part of *[Thirty-six Views of Mount Fuji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty-six_Views_of_Mount_Fuji)*, published by [Nishimuraya Yohachi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nishimuraya_Yohachi) (whose business name was Eijudō). The series began as thirty-six designs and the success forced Hokusai and the publisher to add another ten. The plantation rows turn the foreground into measured horizontal bands, while Fuji sits flat behind."
---

Schongauer's family walks the country road with their bundles, taking the same careful step the engraving does to record them. The peasants are observed, not staged as comedy.

Turner's reapers bend in line across an English estate. Rubens loads a wagon with cut timber and lets the rutted track carry it home. Van Gogh translates Millet's shepherdess into colour from a black-and-white print.

Hokusai turns the tea plantation into rows and lets Fuji stand at the back as one more flat shape. The horses in [Horses in motion](/newsletter/0012-horses-in-motion) charged or paraded. Here the animals walk at work pace.
