Dong Yuan's hemp-fibre brushstrokes became a model for the southern landscape tradition because they do not carve mountains from hard contour. They let moisture gather them slowly out of the paper, as if mist were the first condition of seeing."

    note: "Turner sets fishermen, beached fish and a low sun inside a coastal haze that nearly dissolves the subject. Painted around 1809 and now at the [Barber Institute of Fine Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barber_Institute_of_Fine_Arts) in Birmingham, the picture belongs to his early work in marine atmosphere, before the late storms and fires made dissolution his signature. The title is exact: the sun does not rise over vapour but through it. Light is treated as something that has to push its way across damp air."
  - id: "collection-of-beauty-caspar-david-friedrich-wanderer-above-the-sea-of-fog"
    note: "The figure stands with his back to us on a rock above the [Elbe Sandstone Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elbe_Sandstone_Mountains), looking into a sea of fog. Friedrich painted the work around 1817, using the [Ruckenfigur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%BCckenfigur) device that lets the viewer borrow the figure's position without entering his mind. Peaks break through the vapour like islands, but the main landscape is absence. The fog makes the view grand by withholding it, and the man looks smaller because he is allowed to look so far."
  - id: "collection-of-beauty-monet-w1524"
    note: "Monet painted the Charing Cross Bridge series during London campaigns between 1899 and 1904, often watching the Thames from a balcony at the [Savoy Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savoy_Hotel). In this version, bridge, water, smoke and fog become horizontal bars of mauve and gold. The industrial city gives him the same problem as the lily pond would later: how to paint a subject whose edges are always moving. The canvas is held by the Menard Art Museum in Japan."
  - id: "collection-of-beauty-sailing-boats-mist-hiroshi-yoshida"
    note: "Yoshida's boats drift in a pale sea where mast, sail and mountain all fade at different speeds. Sailing Boats - Mist is one of six variants printed from the same key block in 1926, each state changing the hour or weather through colour and [bokashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bokashi_(printing)) gradation. Yoshida had trained in Western-style painting before turning to woodblock, and he supervised carving and printing closely. The result is technical control made to look like the quiet loss of control that fog brings."
---

Mist is one of landscape's old permissions. It lets a painter leave things unfinished without making them feel absent: riverbanks can soften, mountains can break into islands, and a bridge can become only the memory of a bridge.

This issue moves from ink landscape to Romantic fog, from Turner's damp light to Friedrich's withheld view, then to two prints and paintings where modern weather does the same ancient work. Yoshida's pale sea uses the same soft gradation that Hasui used for rain in [Japanese rain](/newsletter/0002-japanese-rain), but here the water almost stops.
