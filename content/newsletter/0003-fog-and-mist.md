I love paintings where the air does the work. Fog lets the painter leave most of the picture out, and the picture often gets better for it.

Dong Yuan figured this out in China about a thousand years ago, and Chinese landscape painters never let it go. Turner is twenty years from his fire-and-shipwreck phase, but the same instinct is already in his morning vapour. Friedrich hides his mountains and lets you imagine them in. Monet spent five years on the Thames because the fog kept giving him new paintings.

The bokashi gradation Yoshida uses for the fade is the same hand-wiping trick Hasui used for rain in [Japanese rain](/newsletter/0002-japanese-rain). Rain moves, fog waits. On paper they almost match.
