---

Before electricity, night actually looked like this — not a dimmer version of day, but a different world that began at the edge of whatever small light you carried.

The painters in this issue all work that edge. Harunobu's lantern grants the plum blossoms a few feet of existence. La Tour gives Job's whole crisis one candle. Wright of Derby stages science itself around a hidden flame and lets the audience's faces do the arguing. Then the cities arrive: Grimshaw's gaslit Leeds doubles every lamp in wet stone, and Whistler dims London until only the gold flecks remain.

The mirrors in [Mirrors and reflections](/newsletter/0008-mirrors-and-reflections) gave back faces. Night is stingier: it gives back only flames. Painters, sensibly, fell in love with it anyway.
