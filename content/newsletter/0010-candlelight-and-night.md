Before electricity, night actually looked like this. Not a dimmer version of day, but a different world that began at the edge of whatever small light you carried.

The painters here all work that edge. Harunobu's lantern gives the plum blossoms a few feet of existence. La Tour gives Job's whole crisis one candle. Wright of Derby stages a science demonstration around a hidden flame and lets the audience's faces do the arguing. Then the cities arrive: Grimshaw's gaslit Leeds doubles every lamp in wet stone, and Whistler dims London until only a few gold flecks remain.

The mirrors in [Mirrors and reflections](/newsletter/0008-mirrors-and-reflections) gave back faces. Night is stingier. It gives back only flames.
