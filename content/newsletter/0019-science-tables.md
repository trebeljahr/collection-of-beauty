  - id: "collection-of-beauty-de-humani-corporis-fabrica-27"
    note: "A skeleton stands in a landscape, leaning against a spade in the pose of a thinking man rather than a diagram. The woodcut comes from Andreas Vesalius's *[De humani corporis fabrica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_humani_corporis_fabrica)*, the seven-book anatomical treatise published in Basel in 1543, the same year [Copernicus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolaus_Copernicus) published *[De revolutionibus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_revolutionibus_orbium_coelestium)*. The block-cutters were almost certainly Venetian and the designs are sometimes attributed to a member of Titian's workshop, possibly [Jan Stephan van Calcar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Stephen_van_Calcar). The figures recurred throughout sixteenth-century European art as memento-mori as much as anatomical study."
  - id: "collection-of-beauty-the-muscles-of-the-hand-after-albinus-and-of-the-foot-aft-wellcome-v0007837"
    note: "A flayed hand after [Bernhard Siegfried Albinus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernhard_Siegfried_Albinus) and a foot after De Courcelles sit on the same engraved sheet, tendons and small muscles labelled like the parts of a tool. The plate was engraved by [Robert Bénard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_B%C3%A9nard) for the late eighteenth-century editions of the *[Encyclopédie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encyclop%C3%A9die)* edited by [Diderot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denis_Diderot) and [d'Alembert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_le_Rond_d%27Alembert). Bénard cut around 1,800 plates for the *Encyclopédie* over thirty years, working from existing anatomical drawings rather than fresh dissection. The hand sits palm-up like an offering, but it is in fact a study for the engraver who has to give it weight."
  - id: "collection-of-beauty-newton-williamblake"
    note: "William Blake's Newton crouches naked on a rock at the bottom of the sea, bent over a compass and a geometric diagram on a scroll. The image is a colour-printed [monotype](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monotyping) made around 1795 and finished in pen and watercolour, one of the twelve so-called Large Colour Prints. Blake wrote that 'Art is the Tree of Life, Science is the Tree of Death', and Newton with his compass became the emblem of measurement that has stopped seeing. The [Tate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tate)'s copy was given by [W. Graham Robertson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walford_Graham_Robertson) in 1939."
  - id: "collection-of-beauty-johannes-vermeer-the-geographer-google-art-project"
    note: "A geographer in a Japonse rok, the loose imported robe Dutch scholars adopted as study dress, leans over a chart with dividers in his hand and a terrestrial globe behind him. Vermeer painted the canvas in 1669 as the companion piece to *The Astronomer*, signed and dated in two separate places on the cabinet. The sitter for both may be Vermeer's Delft neighbour, the microscopist [Antonie van Leeuwenhoek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonie_van_Leeuwenhoek), though there is no documentary proof. The painting has been at the [Städel Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St%C3%A4del) in Frankfurt since 1885."
  - id: "collection-of-beauty-joseph-wright-of-derby-the-alchemist"
    note: "An aged alchemist kneels before a glowing glass flask as phosphorus ignites in his laboratory. The painting is loosely tied to the Hamburg alchemist [Hennig Brand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hennig_Brand), who isolated [phosphorus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phosphorus) from urine in 1669 while searching for the philosopher's stone, becoming the first European to discover a new chemical element since antiquity. Joseph Wright of Derby painted the canvas in 1771 and reworked it in 1795, and the [Derby Museum and Art Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_Museum_and_Art_Gallery) has owned it since 1883. The flask is set in the centre like an altar lamp."

Vesalius gives the skeleton a landscape and a spade. The body leans against the spade like a man who has just stopped digging.

The hand engraving keeps the person out of the picture and leaves the mechanism for the engraver. Blake bends Newton over a compass at the bottom of a sea. Vermeer puts dividers, a chart and a globe in one quiet room of north light.

The air-pump crowd in [Candlelight and night](/newsletter/0010-candlelight-and-night) watched science as theatre. Wright's alchemist kneels alone before the glowing flask as the phosphorus catches fire.
