The composition adapts antique equestrian models such as the *Marcus Aurelius* in Rome and would set the template for European royal portraiture all the way to Van Dyck and Velázquez. The Russian campaign was already underway when he painted it; by the time of the next Salon, Napoleon was retreating from Moscow and the picture's bravado read as elegy. ---

Uccello covers the ground with broken lances drawn straight, as if the battle is also a geometry lesson. The horses fight and diagram at once.

Titian slows the horse into imperial portraiture. Rubens tangles riders and hounds with two reptiles. Géricault lets a chasseur's mount rear under smoke. Kuniyoshi sends his samurai straight into the current.

The bridges in [Bridges and crossings](/newsletter/0005-bridges-and-crossings) offered a path over water. Here the horse is the path.
