At some point painters discovered the cheapest model available, the mirror, and art history got one of its best genres: artists explaining themselves to themselves, with us eavesdropping.

Dürer at twenty-six performs the artist as gentleman so convincingly that Europe eventually believed him. Goltzius skips his face and draws his burnt working hand instead. Rembrandt brackets a whole life, making faces at the mirror in his twenties and taking honest inventory at fifty-three. And Courbet inflates the self-portrait until it swallows his model, his patrons, Baudelaire and the whole age.

The scientists in [Pictures of science](/newsletter/0019-science-tables) measured the world. The artists, given the same attention to spend, mostly pointed it back at themselves.
