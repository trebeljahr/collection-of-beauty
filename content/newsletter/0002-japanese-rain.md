---

Rain in these prints is drawn, not described. It comes down in parallel cuts, diagonal screens, soaked planes of grey and green. Hiroshige uses it to turn roads, bridges and shrine pines into events of weather.

Hasui inherits that grammar in twentieth-century Tokyo. The city has steel bridges now, and the printing uses softer [bokashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bokashi_(printing)) gradations, but the rain still does the same work. It measures distance, hides detail, holds the passing figure inside the scene.
