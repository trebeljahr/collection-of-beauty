Painting music is a lovely impossible problem: the one thing the picture is about is the one thing it cannot contain. So painters paint everything *around* the sound — the tuning, the listening, the faces — and somehow you hear it anyway.

This issue runs the whole range. Titian's meadow concert is pure mood with the plot left out. Grünewald's angels play for hospital patients who needed the colours as much as the music. Caravaggio paints the moment *before* the music, La Tour paints what musicians do when the music stops paying, and Menzel reconstructs one candlelit evening of it from a century's distance.

Five rooms, five silences, all of them somehow loud.
