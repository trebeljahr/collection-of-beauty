A bridge in a painting is never just infrastructure. It is the line your eye walks across, the proof that two sides of the picture know each other, and usually the place where the story sits.

This issue collects five very different deals with the water. Patinir makes the crossing a one-way moral choice and shrinks the river to a single stride. Carracci builds an honest wooden footbridge and lets it quietly invent landscape painting. Jordaens skips the bridge entirely and overloads the ferry. Whistler dissolves a real London bridge into a memory of Japanese prints — and Constable just stands on the planks he grew up on, watching the work below.

The Hiroshige downpour in [Japanese rain](/newsletter/0002-japanese-rain) hammered a bridge full of running pedestrians. Whistler saw prints like that one and decided London's bridges deserved the same poetry. They do.
