Here's a thing I only noticed slowly: in a lot of great portraits, the person is not the main event. The *cloth* is. Faces take up a few per cent of the canvas; fabric gets the acreage, and painters spent careers learning to make it speak.

This issue listens to five fabrics. The Kaigetsudō kimono performs its wearer entirely. Van Dyck's black silk is wealth made wearable, a dark field he decorates like a jeweller. Delacroix's Algiers interior is pattern as atmosphere — a painting Picasso was still answering 120 years later. Vigée Le Brun's diplomatic silks carried their painter through a revolution, and Goyō closes the issue with the quietest flex of all: transparent cloth, carved into wood.

The mirrors in [Mirrors and reflections](/newsletter/0008-mirrors-and-reflections) doubled people. Cloth does something gentler — it wraps them in a second skin that tells you who they are before their face gets a word in.
