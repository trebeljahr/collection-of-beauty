Before bridges were everywhere, getting across water was an event. You paid someone, you waited for the weather, you trusted a boat or a stranger's shoulders — and painters loved every minute of it, because a crossing is a story with a built-in beginning, middle and far bank.

This issue is five fares. Charon takes souls across a Styx no wider than a stream. Delacroix books you onto the same route in a storm, three centuries on. Jordaens overloads the Antwerp ferry until it nearly ships water, Hiroshige's porters carry the entire Tōkaidō road across a river where boats were forbidden, and Homer ends the trip with one man, one canoe and no particular hurry.

The Hiroshige downpour in [Japanese rain](/newsletter/0002-japanese-rain) caught travellers mid-bridge. Take the bridge away, this issue says, and the water gets personal.
