Before bridges were everywhere, getting across water was an event. You paid someone, you waited for the weather, you trusted a boat or a stranger's shoulders. Painters loved this. A crossing is a story with a built-in beginning, middle and far bank.

This issue is five fares. Patinir's Styx is no wider than a stream. Delacroix books you onto the same route in a storm three centuries later. Jordaens overloads an Antwerp ferry to comic effect. Hiroshige's porters wade the river the shogun forbade boats on, and Homer ends the trip with one man, one canoe, and no particular hurry.

The Hiroshige downpour in [Japanese rain](/newsletter/0002-japanese-rain) caught travellers mid-bridge. Take the bridge away and the water gets a lot more personal.
