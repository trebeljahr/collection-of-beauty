Rogier van der Weyden painted the panel around 1435 to 1438 in his Brussels workshop, where he was the official painter to the city. The picture is known as the Durán Madonna after the Spanish collector José Lázaro Galdiano sold it to the Prado in 1930, where it still hangs.   - id: "collection-of-beauty-giorgione-pala-di-castelfranco"
    note: "Giorgione lifts the Virgin onto a tall throne above Saints Francis and Nicasius, with a Venetian terra firma landscape closing the lower half. He painted the altarpiece around 1503 to 1505 for the chapel of Tuzio Costanzo in the cathedral of Castelfranco Veneto, in memory of Tuzio's son Matteo, who had died fighting in Romagna at twenty-three. Matteo's tomb stands below the altarpiece. The painting has never left Castelfranco except for restoration and the format is a [sacra conversazione](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacra_conversazione), with saints from different centuries sharing one still space."
  - id: "collection-of-beauty-cranach-madonna-under-the-fir-tree"
    note: "Lucas Cranach the Elder places the Virgin and Child against a dark fir rather than an Italianate vista, tightening the picture around green needles and pale skin. He painted the panel around 1510 for Breslau Cathedral, now the cathedral of Wrocław, where it still hangs in the diocesan collection. Cranach had recently become court painter to Friedrich the Wise of Saxony at Wittenberg, and the panel belongs to the spiky verticals of his early court style rather than the rounded landscapes he saw later through Dürer. The fir tree makes the holy pair Saxon, not Mediterranean."
  - id: "collection-of-beauty-parmigianino-madonna-and-child-with-angels-known-as-the-madonna-with-the-long-neck"
    note: "Parmigianino stretches the Virgin's neck, hands and body into long cool curves, and stands a thin column behind her so tall it has no capital. The altarpiece was commissioned in 1534 by Elena Baiardi for her family chapel in Parma and was unfinished when the painter died in 1540 at thirty-seven. The panel passed to the Medici Grand Dukes and is now at the Uffizi. It is a touchstone of Italian [Mannerism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mannerism), with anatomy and architecture deliberately made strange."
  - id: "collection-of-beauty-bartolome-esteban-perez-murillo-008"
    note: "Joseph bends down to play with the infant Jesus and a small goldfinch while Mary pauses at her spinning. Bartolomé Esteban Murillo painted the canvas in Seville around 1645 to 1650 and it entered the Prado from the Spanish royal collection. The goldfinch was a traditional emblem of the Passion to come because the bird is supposed to have plucked a thorn from Christ's crown, but Murillo holds the symbol at the level of a domestic toy. Joseph's tools sit on the floor in the foreground."

Rogier hands the Christ Child a small book and lets him turn the page under a Brussels Gothic canopy. Giorgione lifts the Virgin onto a tall throne above a low Venetian landscape and puts Saints Francis and Nicasius down beside her in a quiet conversation across centuries.

Cranach swaps the Italianate vista for a single dark fir from the Saxon woods. The gold ground of Sansetsu's plum and willow in [Flowers and branches](/newsletter/0007-flowers-and-branches) made the branches shine; these panels use throne, fir and column to change the scale of devotion instead.

Parmigianino stretches the body until the column behind it has no capital. Murillo bends Joseph down to play with the infant Jesus and a small goldfinch on the workshop floor.
