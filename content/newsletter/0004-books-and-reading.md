Someone reading is strangely magnetic to paint — and to look at. The reader is the one person in the picture who is somewhere else entirely, and the painting reorganizes itself around that absence the way a room goes quiet around a person on a phone call.

This issue watches that one act change costume across five centuries. Botticelli and Caravaggio make reading heavy: architecture, skulls, effort. Vermeer turns a letter into a sealed room inside a room. Caillebotte lets reading relax into garden shade, and de Blaas blows the whole privacy open — one page, read aloud over the laundry, the news travelling from hand to voice to courtyard.

Five paintings, one quiet superpower: a few marks on paper that can pull a person clean out of the room they are standing in. Which is also, for what it's worth, what you are doing right now.
