Painters cannot leave ruins alone, and I understand completely. A ruin is proof that the past actually happened, delivered in a form you can walk into — and every century looks at the same broken walls and sees something different.

That's this issue: one motif, five readings. Mantegna props a martyr on the antiquity he loved and excavated. Fuseli's artist takes one look at a colossal marble foot and despairs of ever measuring up. Rubens watches herds wander the palace of the Caesars and finds it restful. Constable, freshly widowed, finds a gutted castle that matches his insides — and Vereshchagin strips the romance off entirely with a fortress whose ruins are still new and whose grass is full of skulls.

From "the past was glorious" to "the past was us": five centuries of painters, circling the same broken stones.
