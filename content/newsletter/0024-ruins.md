---

Ruins keep showing up in painting, and the same broken walls mean very different things in different centuries.

The first four pictures here all find some kind of beauty in decay: antiquity propping up a saint, antiquity humbling an artist, antiquity grazed over by cattle, antiquity used as a metaphor for grief.

Then Vereshchagin breaks the pattern. His ruin is days old, the grass around it is full of skulls, and the romance is gone. The same broken walls Mantegna or Rubens would have painted lovingly look like a crime scene when they are still new. That is the part I keep thinking about.
