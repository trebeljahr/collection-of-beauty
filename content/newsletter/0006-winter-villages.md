    note: "Cargo boats with stern-mounted rudders move through a river that has been slowed by cold. The brushwork is ruled, not freehand: this is [jiehua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiehua), the precise architectural manner Song painters used to render boats and pavilions with engineering accuracy. Guo Zhongshu was a tenth-century official-turned-recluse whose name became a brand attached to many later works.     note: "Skaters and walkers spread across a frozen river while a tilted board, propped with a stick over a scatter of grain, waits for birds at the right edge. Pieter Bruegel the Elder painted the prime version on a small oak panel in 1565, now at the [Royal Museums of Fine Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Museums_of_Fine_Arts_of_Belgium) in Brussels. Around 127 surviving versions are catalogued after this one composition, evidence of how completely the picture became the household winter scene of the Low Countries. The trap is small, but it sits on the same white floor as the games."
  - id: "collection-of-beauty-hendrik-avercamp-winter-scene-on-a-canal-google-art-project"
    note: "Avercamp fills the canal with dozens of small incidents: skaters, sledges, peat carts, gentry chatting with peasants, a fallen figure waiting to be helped up. He was deaf and mute, signed his prints 'de Stomme van Kampen' (the Mute of Kampen), and spent his career in the Hanseatic town of Kampen where the canals froze hard during the [Little Ice Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Ice_Age). The high vantage and low horizon let the crowd spread across the ice without thinning. The frozen river became, in his pictures and in his town, a public room with no roof."

The surface is brushed thinly, with the priming showing through to do the work of frozen ground. The sledge has come to a stop in the middle of the picture, with no figure beside it."

King [Friedrich Wilhelm III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_William_III_of_Prussia) bought the pair for his son the same year, and both are now in the [Alte Nationalgalerie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alte_Nationalgalerie)."
---

Bruegel's village runs trap and game on the same sheet of ice. The skaters do not look at the propped board on the right and the birds have not arrived yet.

Avercamp keeps adding figures until the canal reads as a town square. Rembrandt thins the scene to a sledge stopped in pale light. Guo's boats move through an earlier and colder winter, drawn with a ruler.

Friedrich has no village left. The monks carry a coffin through the snow and the bare oaks stand black against a pale dusk.
