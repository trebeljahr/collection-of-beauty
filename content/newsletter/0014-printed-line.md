Vasari wrote that the boy Michelangelo made a coloured copy of it from a market visit, studying real fish at the stalls so he could colour the scales of the demons correctly; an early Michelangelo painting after this print survives at the Kimbell Art Museum. Lützelburger died in 1526 with the blocks still unpublished, and they finally appeared in Lyon in 1538 from the publishers Melchior and Gaspar Trechsel."

---

Schongauer's demons are line before they are fear, and Vasari's story has young Michelangelo following Schongauer's burin stroke for stroke from a market visit to study real fish scales.

Dürer packs Melencolia's table with measured objects and holds them with hatch. Holbein keeps Death small enough to fit on woodblocks the size of a thumbprint.

The chronicle skeletons came out of Dürer's apprentice workshop a few years after he had left it. A century and a half later Rembrandt sets a single windmill on a town bastion and leaves the paper around it almost empty.
