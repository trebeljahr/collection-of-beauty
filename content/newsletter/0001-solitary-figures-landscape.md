This first issue is about an old trick in landscape painting: put one small figure in a big world and the picture starts asking questions. Where are they going, why alone, and how small are we anyway.

Friedrich's monk stands at the edge of an enormous nothing. Fan Kuan shrinks his travelers until you have to hunt for them under the cliff. Van Gogh's sower walks straight at the sun. Friedrich's shepherd borrows shade from the loneliest tree on the plain, and Hasui's walker crosses the snow of a Tokyo evening leaving the first tracks in it.

None of these figures looks unhappy, mind you. The landscapes are vast and every person in them just keeps walking.
