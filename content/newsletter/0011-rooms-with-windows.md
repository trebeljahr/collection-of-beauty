A room without a window is a box. Cut one opening in the wall and the painting has weather, time of day, an inside and an outside. An entire second world for the price of a rectangle.

The five rooms here all answer to their windows. Vermeer's leaded glass slices daylight into squares and hides a sermon in a roundel. His letter-writer hands the window to the waiting maid for daydreaming. Menzel paints the curtains lifting in an empty room. Van Gogh shutters his window and supplies the light himself, and Lewis turns a Cairo doorway into the sun's own entrance.

After the candles of [Candlelight and night](/newsletter/0010-candlelight-and-night), this issue is the daylight rebuttal. Sometimes you just need to open the curtains.
